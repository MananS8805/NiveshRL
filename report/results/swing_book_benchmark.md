# Swing book: the swing plan as a paper portfolio

₹1,00,000, 1% risk per trade (halved below NIFTY's 200-day average or on narrow breadth), at most 20% of equity per stock and 10 positions, delivery only (no leverage), real delivery costs on every buy and sale. Picks: the point-in-time 'watch for strength' list every 5 trading days, 2017-01-02 → 2026-10-02.

| | CAGR | Sharpe | Max drawdown | Trades | Avg R | Avg invested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swing book | +2.2% | 0.24 | -29.2% | 801 | +0.08 | 76% |
| Same rules on random picks | +1.9% | 0.21 | -40.6% | 844 | +0.08 | 75% |
| NIFTY 50 buy and hold | +10.9% | 0.73 | -38.4% | | | 100% |

Monthly excess return vs NIFTY: -0.75% (90% bootstrap -1.19% to -0.31%); second half (from 2021-12-31): -0.47%/month.
Monthly excess vs the random-pick book: -0.06% (90% bootstrap -0.52% to +0.38%); second half: -0.27%/month.

Most common reasons a pick was not bought: 10 positions already open (1613); not enough cash (908); no open price (12); size rounds to zero (6).
Keeping idle cash in a NIFTY index fund instead of cash (core and satellite) lifts the book to +6.1% a year (Sharpe
0.43, max drawdown −42.1%) and the random-pick book to +4.9%: still well behind holding NIFTY (+10.9%).

**Verdict: the swing plan beats random picks per trade (+0.15R vs +0.08R on all record trades) but, traded as a real
₹1 lakh portfolio with its own sizing rules, it does not beat buying and holding NIFTY 50.** Capital limits force it to
take whichever picks arrive when a slot frees (those averaged +0.08R), a quarter of the money sits idle, and 1% risk
on +0.08R a trade is too little to compound.
